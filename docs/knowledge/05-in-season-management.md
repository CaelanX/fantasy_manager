# 05 - In-season management

Researched 2026-11-10. Every factual claim below links to the page it came from. Anything marked **(inference)** is my own reasoning from the league settings and the sources, not something a source states. Anything marked **(unverified)** could not be confirmed from a primary source.

## What this covers

This document covers what a manager does between the draft and the championship: setting daily lineups, streaming (short-term pickups made for their schedule), working the waiver wire, managing goalies and injuries, judging buy-low and sell-high trades, preparing for the fantasy playoffs, and the daily and weekly routine. It is written for two head-to-head points leagues with tight move limits (ESPN: 2 adds per matchup; Fantrax: 3 per week), so it pays special attention to how a low move limit changes standard advice, most of which is written for leagues with unlimited daily moves.

## Key takeaways

- In a points league with no games-played cap, **points come from games that are actually in an active slot**. A pickup is worth the games it adds to the active lineup, not its per-game average multiplied by its team's game count. Daily Faceoff says a streamer helps on light nights "if you have an open roster spot" and that on busy nights streamers "will have a tough time cracking your lineup" ([Daily Faceoff, Week 6 2026-27](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/fantasy-hockey-2026-27-weekly-strength-of-schedule-and-streaming-targets-week-6)).
- The NHL schedule is lopsided. In 2026-27 Saturdays average almost 13 games, Thursdays about 10, Tuesdays about 9, and Monday, Wednesday, Friday and Sunday about 5 each ([Bangers Fantasy Hockey](https://hockeybangers.substack.com/p/analyzing-the-2026-27-nhl-schedule)). Players on teams with many light-night games therefore fit into lineups more often.
- The spread between teams is large: Washington plays 40 off-night games in 2026-27 and Nashville 22 ([Bangers Fantasy Hockey](https://hockeybangers.substack.com/p/analyzing-the-2026-27-nhl-schedule)).
- ESPN's own strategy writer says limits on roster moves remove most of the off-night streaming edge and force a manager to forecast off-nights a week or more ahead ([ESPN, Sean Allen](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/35196481/espn-nhl-how-add-drop-stream-max-your-team)). With 2 or 3 moves a week, each move must buy several lineup games or a long-term upgrade.
- "Adding a streamer just to put him on your bench ... is a waste of an add" ([DobberHockey, Brudner 2023](https://dobberhockey.com/2023/04/11/eastern-edge-maximizing-the-end-of-the-fantasy-hockey-schedule)).
- Goalies are the only position that can lower a weekly total. Allen's example: five streamed goalies on one night all scored negative, and "being wrong about one goaltender can negate being right about three skaters" ([ESPN, Sean Allen](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/35196481/espn-nhl-how-add-drop-stream-max-your-team)).
- Our two leagues punish goals against very differently. Under the ESPN league's scoring a goalie needs roughly a .882 save percentage to break even before win points; under the Fantrax league's scoring the break-even is .800, so nearly every Fantrax goalie start is positive **(inference, arithmetic shown below)**.
- Real breakouts show up in role and volume first: ice time, power-play role, shot volume and chance quality. A shooting percentage far from a player's norm is the classic sign of a streak that will not last ([Daily Faceoff, According to the Numbers](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/according-to-the-numbers-week-4-fantasy-hockey-pickups-drops-sell-highs-buy-lows); [DobberHockey, Brudner 2024](https://dobberhockey.com/2024/11/03/geek-of-the-week-stock-watch-buy-low-sell-high)).
- Save top waiver priority for a season-changing player; do not spend it on a one-week streamer ([FantasyPros](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/10/how-to-use-the-fantasy-hockey-waiver-wire)).
- An injured player's roster slot is worth his games plus the replacement's games while he is out, which is why an IR slot (which lets both exist at once) is valuable ([DobberHockey, Campkin](https://dobberhockey.com/2014/12/07/geek-of-the-week-dealing-with-injured-players)).
- The 2026-27 NHL season has 84 games per team, ends Saturday 10 April 2027 with all 32 teams playing, and the NHL trade deadline is Monday 1 March 2027 ([The Hockey News](https://thehockeynews.com/news/latest-news/nhl-unveils-2026-27-schedule-key-dates-you-must-circle-on-the-calendar); [Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/2026-27-nhl-trade-deadline-set-monday-march-1-news)).
- Late in the season, comfortable contenders and eliminated teams both rest players; teams still fighting for a playoff spot play their best lineups ([ESPN, Matiash 2025](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/44244119/fantasy-hockey-playoffs-ultimate-strategy-guide)).

## 1. Daily lineup setting

### Games played is the main lever

In a daily-lineup league each active slot can hold one player per day. A *heavy night* is a day when so many NHL teams play that a fantasy team has more players in action than slots; a *light night* (also called an *off-night*) is a day with few NHL games, when slots sit empty. ESPN and theScore both describe Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday as the busy nights in "the vast majority of weeks" ([ESPN, Sean Allen](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/35196481/espn-nhl-how-add-drop-stream-max-your-team); [theScore, Wegman](https://thescore.com/news/1377240)). For 2026-27 the nightly averages are almost 13 games on Saturday, about 10 on Thursday, about 9 on Tuesday and about 5 on each of the other four days ([Bangers Fantasy Hockey](https://hockeybangers.substack.com/p/analyzing-the-2026-27-nhl-schedule)).

Daily Faceoff defines a light night as a day with eight or fewer NHL games in most of its columns ([pre-season 2026-27](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/2026-27-fantasy-hockey-strength-of-schedule); [Week 20, 2025-26](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/fantasy-hockey-2025-26-weekly-strength-of-schedule-and-streaming-targets-week-20)), although its Week 6 column this season used "six or fewer" ([Week 6 2026-27](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/fantasy-hockey-2026-27-weekly-strength-of-schedule-and-streaming-targets-week-6)). The league-wide threshold is only a proxy. What matters for one fantasy team is whether *that team* has an empty slot on that day **(inference)**.

A player whose team plays on light nights is worth more than an identical player whose team plays on heavy nights, because more of his games reach the active lineup. In 2026-27 Washington (40 off-night games, 48% of its schedule), the Rangers (39), Colorado (38) and Utah (37) have the most; Nashville (22), St. Louis (24) and Tampa Bay (25) have the fewest ([Bangers Fantasy Hockey](https://hockeybangers.substack.com/p/analyzing-the-2026-27-nhl-schedule); the same ordering appears in [Daily Faceoff's pre-season schedule piece](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/2026-27-fantasy-hockey-strength-of-schedule)). Both sources warn against over-weighting this: Daily Faceoff treats playoff schedule as a draft-day tiebreaker only, and theScore says a favourable schedule alone should not move a player to the top of a draft board ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/2026-27-fantasy-hockey-strength-of-schedule); [theScore](https://thescore.com/news/1377240)).

### Heavy nights: who sits

On a heavy night the question is which players to bench. The sources I read give signals but no tested weighting:

- **Matchup quality.** Daily Faceoff's weekly tool scores each team's week by games played, opponent difficulty and light nights, and its playoff version uses a "Team Defense Score" so that players with the most games against the weakest defences rank highest ([Week 20, 2025-26](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/fantasy-hockey-2025-26-weekly-strength-of-schedule-and-streaming-targets-week-20); [playoff strength of schedule 2025](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/fantasy-hockey-playoff-strength-of-schedule-week-22-to-24-streaming-options)).
- **Rest and back-to-backs.** A *back-to-back* is two games on consecutive days. Teams win 42.36% of second games of back-to-backs across 9,467 games since 1990-91, about 5 points below their normal rate at home or on the road, and about 76% of those second games are road games ([The Hockey Data Lab](https://thehockeydatalab.blogspot.com/2025/02/back-to-back-games-in-nhl-is-rest-day.html)). This is a blog analysis, not peer-reviewed. It supports a small downgrade for skaters on a tired team and a small upgrade for skaters facing one **(inference)**.
- **Home versus away and betting totals.** A betting *implied team total* is the number of goals the sportsbook market expects a team to score. Search results showed daily-fantasy writers ranking offences by implied total and home teams winning a little over half of games, but I did not read a source that measures how much these should move a season-long start/sit decision, so treat them as tiebreakers **(unverified weighting)**.

The 2026-27 schedule has more back-to-backs for some teams than others: Pittsburgh has 15, five teams have 14, and Vegas and Calgary have 8; 13 of Boston's 14 second legs are on the road ([Bangers Fantasy Hockey](https://hockeybangers.substack.com/p/analyzing-the-2026-27-nhl-schedule)).

### Lineup locks

ESPN's default is "Lock Individually at Scheduled Game Time": each player can be moved between active and bench slots until his own game starts ([ESPN lock times](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360055424451-Lineup-and-Roster-Lock-Times)). The same page says trades, add/drops and IR moves lock at the first scheduled game of the day. The practical consequence is that a late scratch on a 10 pm game can still be swapped for a bench player whose game has not started, but an add may need to be made before the day's first puck drop. Confirm the exact behaviour in the league's own settings page **(unverified for this league)**.

For Fantrax I could not find an official hockey help page. One public Fantrax league's rules page shows daily lineup changes with a lock five minutes before each player's game, but lock rules are a commissioner setting ([example Fantrax rules, MLB league](https://www.fantrax.com/newui/fantasy/gameRules.go?leagueId=6anh3s46iwe1kp9z)). **(unverified for our league)** Correction: for our Fantrax league this is now confirmed. Lineups change daily (the Rules page and the official API show about 194 roster periods inside 27 weekly scoring periods), so reserve players add games and daily lineup setting applies on Fantrax as on ESPN. The exact lock time within the day has not been checked.

## 2. Streaming

*Streaming* means adding a free agent for a short run of games and dropping him afterwards. The standard method, from ESPN's Sean Allen:

1. Set the lineup for the week first and find the days with empty slots.
2. Look at Monday's slate. Teams that play Monday often also play Wednesday and sometimes Friday.
3. Pick free agents from teams whose games land on those light days. In his example, four teams played Monday and Wednesday, and two of them also played Friday and Sunday, making them the best targets ([ESPN, Sean Allen](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/35196481/espn-nhl-how-add-drop-stream-max-your-team)).

Daily Faceoff adds a second pattern: stream a player from one team early in the week, then drop him for a player whose team has its games bunched at the end of the week; in one example this produced four light-night games from a single roster spot ([Week 20, 2025-26](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/fantasy-hockey-2025-26-weekly-strength-of-schedule-and-streaming-targets-week-20); [Week 6 2026-27](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/fantasy-hockey-2026-27-weekly-strength-of-schedule-and-streaming-targets-week-6)). Note that this costs a move at mid-week.

Quantity is not everything. Daily Faceoff notes that some teams with good light-night schedules have few good streaming options because their scoring is concentrated or the team is not scoring ([Week 20, 2025-26](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/fantasy-hockey-2025-26-weekly-strength-of-schedule-and-streaming-targets-week-20)). Allen's rule is that "quantity will usually beat quality" unless the player being replaced is a top-100 player, and that a skater ranked outside the top 100 is usually replaceable ([ESPN, Sean Allen](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/35196481/espn-nhl-how-add-drop-stream-max-your-team)). FantasyPros says the same in looser terms: a four-game player often beats a star who plays twice ([FantasyPros](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/10/how-to-use-the-fantasy-hockey-waiver-wire)).

**Timing.** Brudner recommends making a Friday add on Thursday: set Thursday's lineup, drop a player who is not playing, and claim the target a day early to beat the opponent to him. He also states the core constraint: if the roster is already full on Tuesday or Thursday, an add yields only the off-night games ([DobberHockey, Brudner 2023](https://dobberhockey.com/2023/04/11/eastern-edge-maximizing-the-end-of-the-fantasy-hockey-schedule)).

**Which roster spot.** No source I read names a rule. The logic of the sources implies the streaming spot should be the rostered skater who contributes the fewest lineup points over the rest of the season, which is usually the last bench skater **(inference)**. Allen's warning is the cost side: the only real penalty of streaming is dropping a player "that falls into someone else's lap", so never drop a potential difference-maker to stream ([ESPN, Sean Allen](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/35196481/espn-nhl-how-add-drop-stream-max-your-team)).

**How much is it worth?** I found no published measurement of points gained from streaming in a points league. The sources give only the direction. **(unverified magnitude)** This is a good candidate for the project's own backtest.

## 3. The waiver wire

### Systems

A *waiver* period is a holding time after a player is dropped during which managers submit claims instead of adding him instantly. Under *waiver priority*, claims are awarded in a set order and the winner moves to the bottom ([ESPN waivers overview](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/4669797013268-Waivers-Overview)). Under *FAAB* (free agent acquisition budget; ESPN calls it FAB), each team has a season budget and the highest blind bid wins ([ESPN FAB](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000066231-Free-Agent-Budget-FAB)). Players who clear waivers become *free agents* and can be added first-come, first-served ([ESPN waivers overview](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/4669797013268-Waivers-Overview)).

On ESPN the default waiver period is one day and the process runs daily between 3 and 5 am ET; a player added and dropped on the same day stays a free agent ([ESPN waiver period](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360012531592-Waiver-Period)). On Fantrax, one public league's rules show a minimum of two days on waivers with daily processing, and a FantraxHQ guide gives the FAAB defaults as a $100 budget, $1 increments and a $0 minimum bid; both are commissioner settings ([example Fantrax rules](https://www.fantrax.com/newui/fantasy/gameRules.go?leagueId=6anh3s46iwe1kp9z); [FantraxHQ FAAB guide](https://fantraxhq.com/a-guide-to-using-faab-in-your-fantrax-league/)). **Which system each of our leagues uses must be read from the league settings (unverified).**

### Bidding and priority

- Do not spend top priority on a short-term streamer; hold it for a breakout or an injury replacement who could change the season ([FantasyPros](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/10/how-to-use-the-fantasy-hockey-waiver-wire)).
- The only bid-size guidance I found is from a FantraxHQ soccer guide, so the numbers do not transfer directly: about $1-2 of $100 for a one-week stream, $10-20 to replace a key injured player, $0-1 for a player whose production looks unsustainable, and large bids only for first-round-calibre arrivals. It also advises watching rivals' remaining budgets ([FantraxHQ FAAB guide](https://fantraxhq.com/a-guide-to-using-faab-in-your-fantrax-league/)). I found no hockey-specific study of bid sizes **(unverified for hockey)**.

### Real breakout or noise

Daily Faceoff's weekly method is to compare results with process, looking for players "whose results don't match their process" ([According to the Numbers, Oct 2026](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/according-to-the-numbers-week-4-fantasy-hockey-pickups-drops-sell-highs-buy-lows)). The signals it and DobberHockey use:

- **Role and usage.** A jump in even-strength or power-play ice time, or a move to the top power-play unit (often written PP1), is treated as durable. FantasyPros likewise points to top-six minutes and top power-play time ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/according-to-the-numbers-week-4-fantasy-hockey-pickups-drops-sell-highs-buy-lows); [FantasyPros](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/10/how-to-use-the-fantasy-hockey-waiver-wire)).
- **Shot volume and chance quality.** Shots, shot attempts and individual expected goals (a count of chances weighted by how often such chances score) show whether production can continue ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/according-to-the-numbers-week-4-fantasy-hockey-pickups-drops-sell-highs-buy-lows)).
- **Shooting percentage against career norm.** Brudner projects shot rates to hold and percentages to regress; his sell-high example was a player shooting above 25% with teammates shooting 20.4% while he was on the ice ([DobberHockey, Brudner 2024](https://dobberhockey.com/2024/11/03/geek-of-the-week-stock-watch-buy-low-sell-high)). Daily Faceoff called a 37.5% shooter "a classic hot streak" ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/according-to-the-numbers-week-4-fantasy-hockey-pickups-drops-sell-highs-buy-lows)).
- **Assist luck.** Individual points percentage (the share of on-ice goals a player gets a point on) and on-ice shooting percentage indicate whether assists should rise or fall ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/according-to-the-numbers-week-4-fantasy-hockey-pickups-drops-sell-highs-buy-lows)).
- **Rostered percentage** (the share of leagues in which a player is owned) is used as a price signal: the advice on one pickup was to add him "before the goals start coming and his rostered percentage jumps" ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/according-to-the-numbers-week-4-fantasy-hockey-pickups-drops-sell-highs-buy-lows)).
- **Conditional role checks.** Daily Faceoff made one streamer conditional on still skating with a star centre by Friday, otherwise "leave him on waivers" ([Week 20, 2025-26](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/fantasy-hockey-2025-26-weekly-strength-of-schedule-and-streaming-targets-week-20)). A role has to be re-checked on the day of the add.

For our scoring, a role change that adds shots, hits and blocks has value even without points, because those stats score directly in both leagues **(inference)**.

### Timing of adds

Speed matters ("The managers who act early get them", [FantasyPros](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/10/how-to-use-the-fantasy-hockey-waiver-wire)), but with a move cap the manager cannot chase every lead. The sources support acting early only when the signal is a role change backed by usage, not a scoring streak.

## 4. Goalie management

**Starters are not known far ahead.** Allen writes that a confirmed starter is not certain "and definitely not more than 12 hours in advance" ([ESPN, Sean Allen](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/35196481/espn-nhl-how-add-drop-stream-max-your-team)). Daily Faceoff's starting-goalies page lists each game's projected goalie with a status (the page showed "0 of 28 confirmed" four days ahead) and says it updates live ([Daily Faceoff starting goalies](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/starting-goalies/2026-11-14)). Left Wing Lock publishes a similar page; I could not load it to verify its details.

**Back-to-backs.** Teams usually split a back-to-back between two goalies: the same starter played both games in 35% of back-to-backs in 2000-2009 but only 6.55% in the 2020s ([The Hockey Data Lab](https://thehockeydatalab.blogspot.com/2025/02/back-to-back-games-in-nhl-is-rest-day.html)). So a rostered starter whose team has a back-to-back should be projected for one start, not two, and the backup becomes a predictable one-game stream ([ESPN, Sean Allen](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/35196481/espn-nhl-how-add-drop-stream-max-your-team)). When a goalie does play both, the evidence on performance is mixed. A Hockey Graphs study of about 40,000 games since 1987 found save percentage of .897 in the second game against .905 in the first, with win rate falling from .498 to .421, but the author and commenters note confounders such as road games and backup quality ([Hockey Graphs](https://hockey-graphs.com/2014/12/01/goaltender-performance-vs-rest/)). An NHL.com piece cites Eric Tulsky's 2013 finding of a much larger drop and notes that later work found a smaller effect ([NHL.com, Lukan](https://nhl.com/kraken/news/core-concepts-examining-goalie-workload-329507592)). Season-long fatigue from heavy workloads showed no consistent pattern in a DobberHockey review ([DobberHockey, Laidlaw](https://dobberhockey.com/?p=48859)).

**Sitting a goalie.** Under ESPN's *default* scoring (win 4, save 0.2, goal against -2) a 28-save 3-2 loss scores -0.4 and a 28-save 4-3 win scores 3.6, so "losses hurt" ([ESPN, Matiash 2024](https://africa.espn.com/fantasy/hockey/story/_/id/39491955/fantasy-hockey-pickups-goalies-win-losing)). The same article shows goalies on losing teams who still post positive totals through save volume. Our leagues do not use the default; see the arithmetic in the leagues section.

**Streaming goalies.** Allen's verdict is that it can add points but carries real downside, and that a manager should know the backups, watch back-to-backs, and weigh the opponent ([ESPN, Sean Allen](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/35196481/espn-nhl-how-add-drop-stream-max-your-team)). The Hockey News' weekly primer streams goalies who face the weakest offences ([THN Week 7 primer via Yahoo](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/2026-27-nhl-fantasy-hockey-151902213.html)). Matiash suggests home-only streaming for goalies with strong home splits and using goalies whose teams play on light nights ([ESPN, Matiash 2024](https://africa.espn.com/fantasy/hockey/story/_/id/39491955/fantasy-hockey-pickups-goalies-win-losing)).

**Rotations.** For the fantasy playoffs ESPN recommends carrying four goalies and considering a tandem (both goalies of one NHL team), so one of them plays every game that team plays ([ESPN, Matiash 2025](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/44244119/fantasy-hockey-playoffs-ultimate-strategy-guide)). Our ESPN league caps goalies at three, so the equivalent is a three-goalie rotation over two slots. Even elite starters play only about 90% of their team's games ([ESPN, Sean Allen](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/35196481/espn-nhl-how-add-drop-stream-max-your-team)), so two goalies cannot fill two slots every game day.

## 5. Injuries

An *IR slot* (injured reserve) holds an injured player without using an active or bench spot. On ESPN only players carrying the IR tag are eligible; a healthy player left in an IR slot blocks all adds until he is moved out; and activating him requires an open roster spot ([ESPN IR and waivers](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360035123032-How-does-the-Injured-Reserve-Injury-List-impact-Waivers)). For comparison, Yahoo separates IR (NHL injured reserve only) from IR+ (also day-to-day and out), and likewise blocks adds until a recovered player is activated ([Yahoo help](https://help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN6425.html)). On Fantrax, eligibility and enforcement are commissioner settings; public league rule pages show different choices ([example Fantrax rules](https://www.fantrax.com/newui/fantasy/gameRules.go?leagueId=6anh3s46iwe1kp9z)). **Exactly which injury designations qualify in our two leagues is unverified.**

**Hold or cut.** Campkin's method values the slot, not the player: project the injured player's remaining games, add the best available replacement's production for the games missed, and compare the combined total with alternatives ("you will be getting SOME production from SOME player") ([DobberHockey, Campkin](https://dobberhockey.com/2014/12/07/geek-of-the-week-dealing-with-injured-players)). Cantlon's rule is blunt: do not hold non-stars through long absences, and watch whether a returning player gets his old role back ([Yahoo, Cantlon](https://ca.sports.yahoo.com/news/cut-em-loose-dont-hold-non-stars-injuries-164933532.html)). In redraft leagues, ESPN says not to spend an IR slot on a player who will not return before the fantasy season ends ([ESPN, Matiash 2025](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/44244119/fantasy-hockey-playoffs-ultimate-strategy-guide)).

**IR as an extra roster spot.** An empty IR slot is unused capacity. Search results described adding an IR-tagged player, moving him to IR, and using the freed spot for another add, but I did not read that article in full, and in our leagues each add costs a scarce move **(partly unverified)**.

**Day-to-day players.** With per-player locks, keep a questionable player's replacement on a later game where possible, so a swap is still available when the scratch is announced **(inference from the ESPN lock rule above)**.

## 6. Buy-low and sell-high

*Buy low* means trading for a player whose results are below his underlying play; *sell high* is the reverse. The case for it rests on regression: percentages (individual shooting percentage, on-ice shooting percentage, points percentage) drift back toward a player's norm, while shot volume and ice time are steadier. Both Daily Faceoff and DobberHockey build their lists this way ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/according-to-the-numbers-week-4-fantasy-hockey-pickups-drops-sell-highs-buy-lows); [DobberHockey, Brudner 2024](https://dobberhockey.com/2024/11/03/geek-of-the-week-stock-watch-buy-low-sell-high)).

The case against, or at least the limits:

- Not every cold start is luck. Daily Faceoff separates buy-lows from "Panic Meter" cases where usage and shot output have really fallen: "a real warning sign, not just bad luck" ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/according-to-the-numbers-week-4-fantasy-hockey-pickups-drops-sell-highs-buy-lows)).
- Everything I read is columnist practice. I found no study that tests whether fantasy managers who trade on these signals actually gain points. **(unverified effectiveness)**
- A trade needs a partner who disagrees. If the whole league reads the same columns, the discount may not exist **(inference)**.

Signals to trust most, in order **(inference from the sources' emphasis)**: role and ice time, power-play unit, shot and chance volume, then deviation of percentages from career norms. Points over the last few games alone are the least reliable.

In a points league that pays for shots, hits and blocks, a "sell-high" player with a high volume floor loses less when his percentages regress than a pure scorer does; Brudner makes this point about a high shot, hit and block forward ([DobberHockey, Brudner 2024](https://dobberhockey.com/2024/11/03/geek-of-the-week-stock-watch-buy-low-sell-high)).

## 7. Playoff preparation

**Dates for 2026-27.** The NHL season runs 29 September 2026 to Saturday 10 April 2027, with 84 games per team and all 32 teams playing on the final day ([The Hockey News](https://thehockeynews.com/news/latest-news/nhl-unveils-2026-27-schedule-key-dates-you-must-circle-on-the-calendar)). There is no Olympic break; the All-Star break is 4-7 February and each team has a bye of 9 to 12 days between late January and mid-February ([Bangers Fantasy Hockey](https://hockeybangers.substack.com/p/analyzing-the-2026-27-nhl-schedule)). Yahoo's calendar shows one two-week fantasy week (1-14 February), default playoffs from 22 March to 10 April, and a final week cut short on Saturday 10 April ([Yahoo game dates](https://hockey.fantasysports.yahoo.com/hockey/gamedates)). ESPN's default format in the last two seasons was a two-week first round followed by an 18-day championship round ([ESPN, Matiash 2025](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/44244119/fantasy-hockey-playoffs-ultimate-strategy-guide); [ESPN, Matiash 2026](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/hockey/story/_/id/48184836/espn-nhl-fantasy-hockey-playoffs-h2h-league-tips-picks-strategies)). **The 2026-27 playoff weeks for our ESPN and Fantrax leagues are unverified and must be read from the league settings.**

**Schedule in those weeks.** Assuming Yahoo-style weeks, Washington has 11 games across 22 March-4 April; in the week of 5-10 April, Boston, Dallas, Seattle, Tampa Bay, Utah and Winnipeg play four times while Anaheim, the Rangers, San Jose and Toronto play twice ([Bangers Fantasy Hockey](https://hockeybangers.substack.com/p/analyzing-the-2026-27-nhl-schedule)). Daily Faceoff agrees that Washington and Colorado grade very well and calls the Rangers' playoff schedule "an absolute disaster" ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/2026-27-fantasy-hockey-strength-of-schedule)). These counts come from secondary analyses; recount from the official schedule before acting.

**How to use it.** Daily Faceoff's advice is to add players with a good schedule through the whole playoffs rather than stream week to week, which fits a low move limit, and it flags teams with few light-night games as hard to fit into a lineup ([Daily Faceoff, playoff SOS 2025](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/fantasy-hockey-playoff-strength-of-schedule-week-22-to-24-streaming-options)). ESPN says schedule swaps should be between secondary players of similar value, and that elite players stay put until the very end ([ESPN, Matiash 2025](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/44244119/fantasy-hockey-playoffs-ultimate-strategy-guide)). RotoWire went further for one final week, suggesting dropping stars from a team with no games in the last four days ([RotoWire, Finewax](https://www.rotowire.com/hockey/article/the-week-ahead-the-fantasy-playoffs-91319)); that only makes sense in redraft, in the final matchup.

**Rest risk.** Contenders with nothing to play for and eliminated teams both rest or shut down players; RotoWire listed several stars expected to sit their team's final game ([ESPN, Matiash 2025](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/44244119/fantasy-hockey-playoffs-ultimate-strategy-guide); [RotoWire, Finewax](https://www.rotowire.com/hockey/article/the-week-ahead-the-fantasy-playoffs-91319)). ESPN also notes the opposite opportunity: depth players promoted into top-six or power-play roles on non-contending teams ([ESPN, Matiash 2026](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/hockey/story/_/id/48184836/espn-nhl-fantasy-hockey-playoffs-h2h-league-tips-picks-strategies)).

**Trade deadlines.** The NHL deadline is 1 March 2027 ([Daily Faceoff](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/2026-27-nhl-trade-deadline-set-monday-march-1-news)); real trades change roles and goalie situations. Our leagues' own fantasy trade deadlines are unverified. For keeper leagues, search summaries of DobberHockey columns advise deciding first whether the team is contending or rebuilding and dealing before deadline-week prices rise; I did not read those columns in full **(unverified)**.

## 8. Weekly rhythm

This checklist is assembled from the sources above; the ordering is mine **(inference)**.

- **Sunday night or Monday morning.** Lay out the week: games per team, which days the roster has empty slots, back-to-backs for rostered goalies ([ESPN, Sean Allen](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/35196481/espn-nhl-how-add-drop-stream-max-your-team); [FantasyPros](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/10/how-to-use-the-fantasy-hockey-waiver-wire)). Decide in advance what each move of the week is for.
- **Daily, before the first game.** Check injuries, line and power-play changes, and goalie confirmations; fill every slot that can be filled; resolve heavy-night benching.
- **Daily, near each lock.** Re-check goalie confirmations and late scratches for players not yet locked.
- **Mid-week (Wednesday or Thursday).** Compare the matchup score with the opponent's remaining games and decide whether the remaining moves are needed this week. Make any Friday-targeted add on Thursday ([DobberHockey, Brudner 2023](https://dobberhockey.com/2023/04/11/eastern-edge-maximizing-the-end-of-the-fantasy-hockey-schedule)).
- **Weekly.** Review usage trends (ice time, power-play unit, shots) for rostered players and the top free agents, IR status, and the schedule two to three weeks ahead.

## 9. How a low move limit changes all of this

ESPN's original head-to-head rules allowed an average of one acquisition per NHL game day, about seven per week, and counted only adds: drops and trades do not use the limit ([ESPN hockey rules, older page](https://www.espn.com/espn/print?id=4333164)). Our leagues allow 2 and 3. Consequences, drawn from the sources:

- Off-night streaming as usually described is largely unavailable; additions must be forecast a week or more ahead ([ESPN, Sean Allen](https://africa.espn.com/fhl/story/_/id/35196481/espn-nhl-how-add-drop-stream-max-your-team)).
- A move that yields one lineup game is poor value; a move should be judged by how many lineup games it adds ([DobberHockey, Brudner 2023](https://dobberhockey.com/2023/04/11/eastern-edge-maximizing-the-end-of-the-fantasy-hockey-schedule)).
- Prefer players with good schedules over several weeks to week-by-week streams ([Daily Faceoff, playoff SOS 2025](https://www.dailyfaceoff.com/news/fantasy-hockey-playoff-strength-of-schedule-week-22-to-24-streaming-options)).
- Because drops and trades are free, a two-for-one trade or an IR move can reshape the roster without spending an add, if the league counts moves the way ESPN's rules page describes **(inference; confirm how each league counts a "move")**.
- When not to use a move **(inference)**: when the added player would not start more games than the dropped one; when the matchup is already decided either way and the add has no value beyond this week; when it would leave no move for an injury or a goalie change later in the week; and when the drop is a player with real rest-of-season value.

## How this applies to our two leagues

**Goalie arithmetic (inference from the stated scoring).** Per shot faced at save percentage *p*:

- ESPN league (save 0.2, goal against -1.5): 0.2p - 1.5(1 - p) = 1.7p - 1.5, which is positive only when p > .882. On 30 shots, a .900 night is +0.9 before result points, a .933 night (2 goals) is +2.6, and an .833 night (5 goals) is -2.5. A win adds 2, a shutout 2, an overtime loss 1. Bad starts are mildly negative; a 28-save, 2-goal win is worth 4.6 and a 30-save shutout win 10.
- Fantrax league (save 0.25, goal against -1): 1.25p - 1, positive when p > .800. On 30 shots a .900 night is +3.75 and even an .833 night is +1.25; a win adds 5 and a shutout 7.5.

So in the Fantrax league a confirmed goalie start should essentially always be played and goalie volume (starts and shots faced) is the target. In the ESPN league sitting a goalie is defensible only in a clearly bad matchup, and with three goalies for two slots the choice arises mainly on days when all three start.

**ESPN (14 teams, 9 F / 5 D / 1 UTIL / 2 G, 5 bench, 2 IR, 2 adds per matchup).** There are 22 non-IR roster spots (17 active plus 5 bench). With three goalies that leaves 19 skaters for 15 skater slots, so the skater bench is four deep (five with only two goalies). On light nights, when only about 5 NHL games (10 of 32 teams) are played, many of the 15 slots will be empty, and that cannot be fixed by streaming at two adds a week. The durable fix is roster composition: prefer skaters from teams with many off-night games when values are close. Five defence slots make blocks (0.5) and shots matter, and a 14-team league has a thin free-agent pool, so a dropped player is likely to be claimed.

**Fantrax (12 teams, 6 F / 4 D / 2 G, up to 6 reserve but 4 in practice under the 16-player cap, 2 IR, 3 moves per week, daily lineups, keep 3 per team).** There are only ten skater slots. With the stated 16-player maximum and two or three goalies, that means 13 or 14 skaters for ten slots, a higher bench-to-slot ratio than the ESPN league, so heavy-night conflicts are more frequent and light nights are easier to fill **(inference)**. The lineup problem is more often choosing the best ten each day, so per-game quality and matchup matter relatively more than raw games. Only a team's top 3 players carry over, so long-term value limits who can be dropped for a streamer only for those keepers; the streaming spot should be a reserve player with no long-term value. High shot (0.5) and goal (4) weights reward volume shooters.

## Implications for the engine

Well supported by sources:

1. **Value a pickup by marginal lineup points.** Simulate the daily lineup for the rest of the matchup (and a longer horizon) with and without the add; the value is the difference in started points. Do not use per-game value times team games.
2. **Compute slot conflicts per day for this roster**, not league-wide light nights. Use league-wide counts only as a fallback.
3. **Never recommend an add whose games in the current matchup would all sit on the bench**, unless it is flagged as a long-term hold.
4. **Project one start, not two, for a starter on a back-to-back**, and treat the backup as a likely starter for one game.
5. **Treat goalie starts as uncertain until confirmed**; show the confirmation status and re-run goalie decisions close to lock.
6. **Charge a move cost.** With N moves per period, an add must beat a threshold, and the last remaining move should carry a reserve value for injuries until late in the period.
7. **Separate usage signals from results** in pickup ranking: ice time, power-play unit, shots and chances first; flag shooting percentage far above career norm as regression risk.
8. **Block adds when a healthy player sits in an ESPN IR slot**, and prompt the IR move first.
9. **Playoff planning.** Weight games in the league's actual playoff weeks, discount players on teams likely to rest or shut down players, and prefer multi-week schedule holds.

Own inference, to be tested:

10. **Scoring-specific goalie rule.** Start every confirmed goalie in the Fantrax league; in the ESPN league start unless expected points are negative, using break-even save percentage .882.
11. **Off-night roster value.** Add a schedule term to rest-of-season value equal to expected started games given this roster's slot conflicts.
12. **Free moves.** Model drops, trades and IR moves as not consuming the add limit only after confirming how each league counts moves.
13. **End-of-matchup rule.** If win probability for the matchup is already near 0 or 1, spend remaining moves only on rest-of-season value.
14. **Injury hold test.** Hold an injured player when his projected value after return plus replacement value while out beats the best free agent over the same horizon; in redraft, cut if he will not return before the league's final week.
15. **Backtest streaming value** to replace the missing published number.

## Open questions / where sources disagree

- **Light-night threshold.** Daily Faceoff uses eight or fewer games in most pieces but six or fewer in its Week 6 2026-27 column.
- **Goalie back-to-back effect.** Tulsky's large drop, as reported by NHL.com, against smaller effects in later work and a .008 gap in the Hockey Graphs data.
- **Quantity versus quality.** Allen says quantity usually wins below the top 100; Daily Faceoff repeatedly downgrades teams with games but weak offence. No source gives a number.
- **Streaming value in points leagues** is not quantified anywhere I looked.
- **Buy-low/sell-high effectiveness** is asserted, not tested.
- **League-specific unknowns (most now known, see the [README](README.md#league-facts-confirmed-from-the-app)):** waivers are ESPN priority with a 24-hour hold after a drop, and Fantrax free agents first come first served with waivers 2 days in priority order; the ESPN trade deadline is 2027-02-26 and Fantrax has none; playoffs are periods 25 to 27 in both leagues; Fantrax lineups change daily. Still unknown: IR eligibility rules (Fantrax), what counts as a "move", and exact lock behaviour for ESPN adds.
- **Home/away and betting-total adjustments** for skater start/sit have no verified size.
